APPENDIX C · THEORETICAL PROOFS · THEOREM 3.1

Weak convergence

#

Theorem 3.1 · Weak convergence of positive and negative class-conditional statistical invariants

Under the above conditions, the empirical invariants converge uniformly to their population counterparts. Specifically, for the positive class, as \(l_1\to\infty\), \[\begin{equation} \sup_{f_+\in\mathcal F_{+}} \sup_{\phi_+\in\mathscr P_+} \left| \widehat{\mathcal S}_{+,l_1}(f_+,\phi_+) - \mathcal S_+(f_+,\phi_+) \right| \xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}0, \tag{13} \end{equation}\] whereas for the negative class, as \(l_2\to\infty\), \[\begin{equation} \sup_{f_-\in\mathcal F_{-}} \sup_{\phi_-\in\mathscr P_-} \left| \widehat{\mathcal S}_{-,l_2}(f_-,\phi_-) - \mathcal S_-(f_-,\phi_-) \right| \xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}0. \tag{14} \end{equation}\]

Complete proof

Proof. We first establish the weak convergence of the positive class-conditional statistical invariants. Since \(y_{+,i}=1\) for all \(i=1,\ldots,l_1\), Eqs. (9) and (10) give, for any \(f_+\in\mathcal F_+\) and \(\phi_+\in\mathscr P_+\), \[\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}&\widehat{\mathcal S}_{+,l_1}(f_+, \phi_+) -\mathcal S_+(f_+, \phi_+)\\ &= \bigl\langle f_+, \frac{1}{l_1} \sum_{i=1}^{l_1} \phi_+(x_{+,i})K(x_{+,i},\cdot) - \int_{\mathcal X} \phi_+(x)K(x,\cdot)\,dP_+(x) \bigr\rangle_{\mathcal H}\\ &\quad - \bigl[ \frac{1}{l_1} \sum_{i=1}^{l_1} \phi_+(x_{+,i}) - \int_{\mathcal X} \phi_+(x)\,dP_+(x) \bigr],\end{aligned}\tag{C.1}\end{equation}\] where the first equality follows from the reproducing property \(f_+(x)=\langle f_+,K(x,\cdot)\rangle_{\mathcal H}\). Hence, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, \[\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} &\left| \widehat{\mathcal S}_{+,l_1}(f_+,\phi_+) -\mathcal S_+(f_+,\phi_+) \right|\\ &\leq \|f_+\|_{\mathcal H} \left\| \frac{1}{l_1}\sum_{i=1}^{l_1} \phi_+(x_{+,i})K(x_{+,i},\cdot) - \int_{\mathcal X} \phi_+(x)K(x,\cdot)\,dP_+(x) \right\|_{\mathcal H}\\ &\quad+ \left| \frac{1}{l_1}\sum_{i=1}^{l_1}\phi_+(x_{+,i}) - \int_{\mathcal X}\phi_+(x)\,dP_+(x) \right|. \end{aligned} \tag{C.2} \end{equation}\]

Since \(\|K(x,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal H}=\sqrt{K(x,x)}\leq\kappa\) and the predicates in \(\mathscr P_+\) are uniformly bounded, both \(\phi_+(x)K(x,\cdot)\) and \(\phi_+(x)\) are integrable. For each fixed \(\phi_+\in\mathscr P_+\), the samples \(x_{+,1},\ldots,x_{+,l_1}\) are i.i.d. from \(P_+\). Therefore, the Hilbert-space and scalar strong laws of large numbers imply, respectively, \[\begin{equation}\bigl\| \frac{1}{l_1} \sum_{i=1}^{l_1} \phi_+(x_{+,i})K(x_{+,i},\cdot) - \int_{\mathcal X} \phi_+(x)K(x,\cdot)\,dP_+(x) \bigr\|_{\mathcal H} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}0\tag{C.3}\end{equation}\] and \[\begin{equation}\bigl| \frac{1}{l_1} \sum_{i=1}^{l_1}\phi_+(x_{+,i}) - \int_{\mathcal X}\phi_+(x)\,dP_+(x) \bigr| \xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}0.\tag{C.4}\end{equation}\] Taking the supremum over \(\mathcal F_+\) and \(\mathscr P_+\) in Eq. (C.2) yields \[\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}&\sup_{f_+\in\mathcal F_+} \sup_{\phi_+\in\mathscr P_+} \bigl| \widehat{\mathcal S}_{+,l_1}(f_+, \phi_+) -\mathcal S_+(f_+, \phi_+) \bigr|\\ &\leq \bigl( \sup_{f_+\in\mathcal F_+}\|f_+\|_{\mathcal H}\bigr) \max_{\phi_+\in\mathscr P_+} \bigl\| \frac{1}{l_1} \sum_{i=1}^{l_1} \phi_+(x_{+,i})K(x_{+,i},\cdot) - \int_{\mathcal X} \phi_+(x)K(x,\cdot)\,dP_+(x) \bigr\|_{\mathcal H}\\ &\quad + \max_{\phi_+\in\mathscr P_+} \bigl| \frac{1}{l_1} \sum_{i=1}^{l_1} \phi_+(x_{+,i}) - \int_{\mathcal X} \phi_+(x)\,dP_+(x) \bigr|.\end{aligned}\tag{C.5}\end{equation}\] By assumption, \(\sup_{f_+\in\mathcal F_+}\|f_+\|_{\mathcal H}<\infty\). Moreover, since \(\mathscr P_+\) is finite, the two maxima converge to zero almost surely on a common probability-one event. Therefore, the empirical positive class-conditional statistical invariants converge uniformly to their population counterparts almost surely, establishing Eq. (13).

The weak convergence of the negative class-conditional statistical invariants follows analogously. Since \(y_{-,i}=1\), the samples \(x_{-,1},\ldots,x_{-,l_2}\) are i.i.d. from \(P_-\), and \(\mathcal F_-\) and \(\mathscr P_-\) satisfy the same boundedness and finiteness conditions, the same argument yields Eq. (14). ◻

APPENDIX D · THEORETICAL PROOFS · PROPOSITION 3.1

Structural relationships

#

Proposition 3.1 · Structural relationships of TLUSI

Consider the TLUSI problems in Eqs. (15) and (16). Under appropriate choices of \(\mathcal P_+\) and \(\mathcal P_-\), TLUSI exhibits the following structural relationships with representative twin SVM models:

  1. If \(\mathcal P_+=\mathbf{I}_+\) and \(\mathcal P_-=\mathbf{I}_-\), TLUSI can be transformed into the corresponding TBSVM formulation [40].

  2. If \(\mathcal P_+=\mathbf{D}_+\) and \(\mathcal P_-=\mathbf{D}_-\), where \(\mathbf{D}_+\) and \(\mathbf{D}_-\) are the within-class local weighting matrices, and the inter-class neighborhood matrices satisfy \(\mathbf{F}_+=\mathbf{I}_+\) and \(\mathbf{F}_-=\mathbf{I}_-\), TLUSI has the same principal optimization structure as the corresponding WTSVM formulation [41].

  3. Let \(\boldsymbol\Sigma_+,\boldsymbol\Sigma_-\in \mathbb R^{l\times l}\) be positive-semidefinite within-class structural matrices, and define \(\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_\pm =\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol\Sigma_\pm,0)\). Suppose that \((\mathbf H_+^\dagger\mathbf H_+)\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_+ =\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_+\) and \((\mathbf H_-^\dagger\mathbf H_-)\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_- =\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_-,\) where \(\mathbf H_\pm^\dagger\) denote the Moore–Penrose inverses. If \(\mathcal P_+=\mathbf I_+ +\lambda_+(\mathbf H_+^\dagger)^\top \widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_+\mathbf H_+^\dagger\) and \(\mathcal P_-=\mathbf I_- +\lambda_-(\mathbf H_-^\dagger)^\top \widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_-\mathbf H_-^\dagger\), with \(\lambda_+,\lambda_-\geq0\), the TLUSI invariant terms decompose into within-class squared fitting and structural regularization terms, reducing TLUSI to the corresponding STWSVM formulation [42].

Complete proof

Proof. Consider the TLUSI problems in Eqs. (15) and (16). It suffices to analyze the positive problem, since the negative result follows by interchanging \((\mathbf{X}_+,l_1,c_1,c_3)\) and \((\mathbf{X}_-,l_2,c_2,c_4)\). We observe that \(\mathbf H_+\mathbf u_1 =K(\mathbf X_+,\mathbf X)\mathbf w_1+b_1\mathbf e_1\), and \(\mathbf y_+=\mathbf e_1,\) where \(\mathbf w_1\in\mathbb R^l\) is the kernel expansion coefficient vector. By introducing the translated bias \(\widetilde b_1=b_1-1\) and defining \(\widetilde{\mathbf u}_1= [\mathbf w_1^\top,\widetilde b_1]^\top\), the invariant term can be written as \[\begin{equation}(\mathbf{H}_+\mathbf{u}_1-\mathbf{y}_+)^\top\mathcal{P}_+(\mathbf{H}_+\mathbf{u}_1-\mathbf{y}_+) =\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_1^\top\bigl(\mathbf{H}_+^\top\mathcal{P}_+\mathbf{H}_+\bigr)\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_1.\tag{D.1}\end{equation}\] and the constraint becomes \(\mathbf H_-\widetilde{\mathbf u}_1-\boldsymbol\xi_2 \leq-\tfrac12\mathbf e_2.\) Introduce the invertible transformation \[\begin{equation*}\overline{\mathbf w}_1=2\mathbf w_1,\quad \overline b_1=2\widetilde b_1,\quad \overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2=2\boldsymbol\xi_2,\quad \overline{\mathbf u}_1= [\overline{\mathbf w}_1^\top,\overline b_1]^\top.\end{equation*}\] Multiplying the transformed objective by four and removing the variable-independent constant yields the equivalent problem \[\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} \min_{\overline{\mathbf u}_1,\overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2}\quad& \frac{c_3}{2}\overline{\mathbf u}_1^\top \mathbf H_+^\top\mathcal P_+\mathbf H_+ \overline{\mathbf u}_1 +\frac12\|\overline{\mathbf u}_1\|_2^2 +2\overline b_1 +2c_1\mathbf e_2^\top\overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2 \\ \mathrm{s.t.}\quad& -\mathbf H_-\overline{\mathbf u}_1 +\overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2\geq\mathbf e_2, \overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2\geq\mathbf0. \end{aligned} \tag{D.2} \end{equation}\]

Thus, the three relationships in Proposition 3.1 differ only in the specification of \(\mathcal P_+\).

(i) Relationship with TBSVM.

When \(\mathcal P_+=\mathbf I_+\), the quadratic invariant term in Eq. (D.2) reduces to \[\begin{equation}\overline{\mathbf u}_1^\top \mathbf H_+^\top\mathbf H_+\overline{\mathbf u}_1 =\|\mathbf H_+\overline{\mathbf u}_1\|_2^2 =\|K(\mathbf X_+,\mathbf X)\overline{\mathbf w}_1 +\overline b_1\mathbf e_1\|_2^2.\tag{D.3}\end{equation}\] Setting \(C_1=2c_1\), Eq. (D.2) becomes \[\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} \min_{\overline{\mathbf u}_1,\overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2}\quad& \frac{c_3}{2}\|\mathbf H_+\overline{\mathbf u}_1\|_2^2 +\frac12\|\overline{\mathbf u}_1\|_2^2 +2\overline b_1 +C_1\mathbf e_2^\top\overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2 \\ \mathrm{s.t.}\quad& -\mathbf H_-\overline{\mathbf u}_1 +\overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2\geq\mathbf e_2, \overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2\geq\mathbf0. \end{aligned} \tag{D.4} \end{equation}\] Except for the linear bias correction term \(2\bar{b}_1\) induced by the probability calibration (\(b_1 = \widetilde{b}_1+1\)), Eq. (D.4) is mathematically identical to the primal TBSVM formulation. Hence, TLUSI and TBSVM have the same principal optimization structure.

(ii) Relationship with WTSVM.

When \(\mathcal P_+=\mathbf D_+ =\operatorname{diag}(d_1^+,\ldots,d_{l_1}^+)\), with \(d_i^+\geq0\), and the cross-class neighborhood matrix satisfies \(\mathbf{F}_-=\mathbf{I}_-\), the quadratic invariant term in Eq. (D.2) expands to \[\begin{equation}\overline{\mathbf u}_1^\top \mathbf H_+^\top\mathbf D_+\mathbf H_+ \overline{\mathbf u}_1 = \sum_{i=1}^{l_1}d_i^+ \bigl(K(\mathbf x_{+,i},\mathbf X)\overline{\mathbf w}_1 +\overline b_1\bigr)^2 =\|\mathbf D_+^{1/2}\mathbf H_+ \overline{\mathbf u}_1\|_2^2.\tag{D.5}\end{equation}\] Consequently, Eq. (D.2) becomes \[\begin{equation} \begin{aligned} \min_{\overline{\mathbf u}_1,\overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2}\quad& \frac{c_3}{2}\|\mathbf D_+^{1/2}\mathbf H_+ \overline{\mathbf u}_1\|_2^2 +\frac12\|\overline{\mathbf u}_1\|_2^2 +2\overline b_1 +C_1\mathbf e_2^\top\overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2 \\ \mathrm{s.t.}\quad& -\mathbf F_-\mathbf H_-\overline{\mathbf u}_1 +\overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2\geq\mathbf e_2, \overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2\geq\mathbf0. \end{aligned} \tag{D.6} \end{equation}\] With \(C_1 = 2c_1\), Eq (D.6) therefore has the same structure as the corresponding WTSVM problem up to the linear bias correction \(2\bar{b}_1\).

(iii) Relationship with STWSVM.

When \(\mathcal P_+=\mathbf I_+ +\lambda_+(\mathbf H_+^\dagger)^\top \widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_+\mathbf H_+^\dagger\), the matrix \(\mathbf H_+^\dagger\mathbf H_+\) is the orthogonal projector onto the row space of \(\mathbf H_+\). The assumed compatibility condition, together with the symmetry of \(\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_+\), implies \[\begin{equation}(\mathbf H_+^\dagger\mathbf H_+)^\top \widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_+ (\mathbf H_+^\dagger\mathbf H_+) =\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_+.\tag{D.7}\end{equation}\] Therefore, \[\begin{equation}\mathbf H_+^\top\mathcal P_+\mathbf H_+ =\mathbf H_+^\top\mathbf H_+ +\lambda_+(\mathbf H_+^\dagger\mathbf H_+)^\top \widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_+ (\mathbf H_+^\dagger\mathbf H_+) =\mathbf H_+^\top\mathbf H_+ +\lambda_+\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_+.\tag{D.8}\end{equation}\] Since \(\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_+ =\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol\Sigma_+,0)\), the invariant term decomposes as \[\begin{equation}\overline{\mathbf u}_1^\top \mathbf H_+^\top\mathcal P_+\mathbf H_+ \overline{\mathbf u}_1 =\|\mathbf H_+\overline{\mathbf u}_1\|_2^2 +\lambda_+\overline{\mathbf w}_1^\top \boldsymbol\Sigma_+\overline{\mathbf w}_1.\tag{D.9}\end{equation}\] Substituting Eq. (D.9) into Eq. (D.2) and dividing the objective by \(c_3>0\) yields \[\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}\min_{\overline{\mathbf u}_1,\overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2}\quad & \frac12\|\mathbf H_+\overline{\mathbf u}_1\|_2^2 + \frac{\lambda_+}{2}\overline{\mathbf w}_1^\top \boldsymbol\Sigma_+\overline{\mathbf w}_1 + \frac{1}{2c_3}\|\overline{\mathbf u}_1\|_2^2 + \frac{2c_1}{c_3}\mathbf e_2^\top \overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2 + \frac{2}{c_3}\overline b_1 \\ \mathrm{s.t.}\quad & -\mathbf H_-\overline{\mathbf u}_1 +\overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2\geq\mathbf e_2, \overline{\boldsymbol\xi}_2\geq\mathbf0.\end{aligned}\tag{D.10}\end{equation}\] By matching parameters \(\rho_1=2c_1/c_3\), \(\rho_2=1/c_3\), and \(\rho_3=\lambda_+\), this matches the primal formulation of STWSVM, subject to the linear bias correction \((2/c_3)\overline b_1\).

The same arguments apply symmetrically to the negative TLUSI problem with \(\mathcal P_-\), \(\mathbf D_-\), \(\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_-\), and the corresponding negative parameters. This completes the proof of Proposition 3.1. ◻

APPENDIX E · THEORETICAL PROOFS · THEOREM 3.2

Proximal-SOR convergence

#

Theorem 3.2 · Convergence of the TLUSI Dual Optimization Algorithm

Let \(\Psi(\mathbf{z})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{z}^\top\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{g}^\top\mathbf{z}, ~~ \mathcal Z=[0,C]^n,\) where \(\mathbf{Q}\succeq\mathbf{0}\) and \(C>0\). Suppose that \(0<\omega<2\) and that \(\{\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\}\) is the sequence generated by Algorithm 1 without premature termination. Then:

  1. \(\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\in\mathcal Z\) for every \(k\), and \(\{\Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k)})\}\) converges monotonically to the optimal objective value;

  2. \[\begin{equation*}\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)}-\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\|_2=0, \lim_{k\to\infty}s_k=0;\end{equation*}\]

  3. every accumulation point of \(\{\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\}\) solves Eq. (21), and \[\begin{equation*}\lim_{k\to\infty} \operatorname{dist}\!\left(\mathbf{z}^{(k)},\mathcal Z^*\right)=0,\quad \mathcal Z^*=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal Z}\Psi(\mathbf{z}).\end{equation*}\]

If \(\mathbf{Q}\succ\mathbf{0}\), the optimal solution is unique and the entire sequence converges to that solution.

Complete proof

Proof. (i) Since \(\mathbf{z}^{(0)}=\mathbf{0}\in\mathcal Z\), let \(\mathbf{z}^{(k,i-1)}\) denote the intermediate vector before coordinate \(i\) is updated during sweep \(k\), with \(\mathbf{z}^{(k,0)}=\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\) and \(\mathbf{z}^{(k,n)}=\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)}\). From Algorithm 1, \[\begin{equation}r_i = \sum_{j<i}Q_{ij}z_j^{(k+1)} + \sum_{j\geq i}Q_{ij}z_j^{(k)} -g_i =\nabla_i\Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k,i-1)}).\tag{E.1}\end{equation}\] Hence the coordinate update is equivalently \[\begin{equation} z_i^{(k,i)} = \Pi_{[0,C]} \left( z_i^{(k,i-1)} - \frac{\omega} {Q_{ii}+\rho} \nabla_i\Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k,i-1)}) \right), \tag{E.2} \end{equation}\] while all other coordinates remain unchanged. Since \(\mathbf Q\succeq\mathbf0\) gives \(Q_{ii}\geq0\) and \(\rho\geq\epsilon>0\) by construction, the denominator is strictly positive. The projection therefore guarantees \(\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\in\mathcal Z\) for every \(k\). Let \(\Delta_i^{(k)} = z_i^{(k,i)}-z_i^{(k,i-1)}.\) The variational inequality for the projection in Eq. (E.2), evaluated at \(z_i^{(k,i-1)}\in[0,C]\), gives \[\begin{equation} \nabla_i\Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k,i-1)})\Delta_i^{(k)} + \frac{Q_{ii}+\rho}{\omega} |\Delta_i^{(k)}|^2 \leq0. \tag{E.3} \end{equation}\] Since \(\Psi\) is quadratic and only coordinate \(i\) changes, \[\begin{equation}\Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k,i)}) = \Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k,i-1)}) \quad+ \nabla_i\Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k,i-1)})\Delta_i^{(k)} + \frac{Q_{ii}}{2}|\Delta_i^{(k)}|^2.\tag{E.4}\end{equation}\] Combining Eqs. (E.3) and (E.4) yields \[\begin{equation}\Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k,i-1)}) - \Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k,i)}) \geq \left( \frac{Q_{ii}+\rho}{\omega} - \frac{Q_{ii}}{2} \right) |\Delta_i^{(k)}|^2.\tag{E.5}\end{equation}\] Because \(0<\omega<2\), we have \[\begin{equation}\frac{Q_{ii}+\rho}{\omega} -\frac{Q_{ii}}{2} = Q_{ii}\left(\frac{1}{\omega}-\frac{1}{2}\right) +\frac{\rho}{\omega} >0.\tag{E.6}\end{equation}\] Since the number of coordinates is finite, there exists \(\eta>0\) such that, after summing Eq. (E.5) over one complete sweep, \[\begin{equation} \Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k)}) - \Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)}) \geq \eta \sum_{i=1}^{n}|\Delta_i^{(k)}|^2 = \eta \|\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)}-\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\|_2^2. \tag{E.7} \end{equation}\] Thus \(\{\Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k)})\}\) is nonincreasing. Since \(\mathcal Z\) is compact and \(\Psi\) is continuous, the objective is bounded below on \(\mathcal Z\) and therefore converges to a finite limit. We show in part (iii) that this limit is the optimal objective value.

(ii) Summing Eq. (E.7) over \(k\) gives \[\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}&\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \|\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)}-\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\|_2^2 <\infty.\end{aligned}\tag{E.8}\end{equation}\] Consequently, \[\begin{equation} \|\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)}-\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\|_2 \longrightarrow0. \tag{E.9} \end{equation}\] In particular, since \[\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}&d_k= \frac{\|\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)}-\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\|_2} {1+\|\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\|_2},\end{aligned}\tag{E.10}\end{equation}\] we also have \(d_k\to0\). The convergence \(s_k\to0\) follows below from the optimality of all accumulation points.

(iii) Since \(\{\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\}\subset\mathcal Z\) and \(\mathcal Z\) is compact, the sequence has an accumulation point. Let \(\mathbf{z}^{(k_j)}\longrightarrow\bar{\mathbf{z}}.\) By Eq. (E.9) and Eq. (E.7), each coordinate change \(\Delta_i^{(k_j)}\) tends to zero. Hence every intermediate vector \(\mathbf{z}^{(k_j,i)}\) in the same sweep also converges to \(\bar{\mathbf{z}}\). Passing to the limit in Eq. (E.2) gives, for each \(i=1,\ldots,n\), \[\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}&\bar z_i = \Pi_{[0,C]} \bigl( \bar z_i - \frac{\omega}{Q_{ii}+\rho} \nabla_i\Psi(\bar{\mathbf{z}}) \bigr).\end{aligned}\tag{E.11}\end{equation}\] Since \(\omega/(Q_{ii}+\rho)>0\), this fixed-point relation is equivalent to \[\begin{equation}\begin{cases} \nabla_i\Psi(\bar{\mathbf{z}})\geq0, & \bar z_i=0,\\[1mm] \nabla_i\Psi(\bar{\mathbf{z}})=0, & 0<\bar z_i<C,\\[1mm] \nabla_i\Psi(\bar{\mathbf{z}})\leq0, & \bar z_i=C. \end{cases}\tag{E.12}\end{equation}\] These are exactly the first-order optimality conditions for minimizing the convex function \(\Psi\) over \(\mathcal Z\). Therefore, \(\bar{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathcal Z^*\). Since the same argument applies to every convergent subsequence, every accumulation point of \(\{\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\}\) is globally optimal.

Because \(\Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k)})\) has a limit and \(\mathbf{z}^{(k_j)}\to\bar{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathcal Z^*\), continuity gives \[\begin{equation}\begin{aligned}&\Psi(\mathbf{z}^{(k_j)}) \longrightarrow \Psi(\bar{\mathbf{z}}) = \min_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal Z}\Psi(\mathbf{z}).\end{aligned}\tag{E.13}\end{equation}\] Hence the entire objective sequence converges to the optimal objective value, which completes part (i).

We next show convergence to the solution set. If \(\operatorname{dist} \left(\mathbf{z}^{(k)},\mathcal Z^*\right) \not\longrightarrow0,\) then there exist \(\delta>0\) and a subsequence satisfying \(\operatorname{dist} \left(\mathbf{z}^{(k_j)},\mathcal Z^*\right) \geq\delta.\) By compactness, this subsequence has a further convergent subsequence. Its limit is an accumulation point of \(\{\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\}\) and hence belongs to \(\mathcal Z^*\), which contradicts the inequality above. Therefore, \[\begin{equation} \operatorname{dist} \left(\mathbf{z}^{(k)},\mathcal Z^*\right) \longrightarrow0. \tag{E.14} \end{equation}\] For every \(\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal Z^*\), the first-order optimality condition is equivalently \(\mathbf{z} = \Pi_{\mathcal Z} \bigl( \mathbf{z}-\mathbf Q\mathbf{z}+\mathbf g \bigr).\) The projected-gradient residual used in Algorithm 1 is continuous. Since Eq. (E.14) holds and this residual vanishes on \(\mathcal Z^*\), \[\begin{equation}s_k = \bigl\| \mathbf{z}^{(k+1)} - \Pi_{\mathcal Z} \bigl( \mathbf{z}^{(k+1)} - \mathbf Q\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)} + \mathbf g \bigr) \bigr\|_\infty \longrightarrow0.\tag{E.15}\end{equation}\] This completes part (ii).

Finally, if \(\mathbf Q\succ\mathbf0\), then \(\Psi\) is strictly convex on \(\mathcal Z\), so \(\mathcal Z^*\) contains a unique minimizer. Since every accumulation point of \(\{\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\}\) equals this minimizer, the entire sequence converges to the unique optimal solution. ◻
